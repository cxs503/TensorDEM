Inﬂuence of the local friction coeﬃcient The local friction coeﬃcient µ has a signiﬁcant inﬂu- ence on the dilatancy law. The solid fraction remains a linearly decreasing function of I, but both parameters νmax and a depend on µ. The variation of a is not simple: we measure a = 0.38 for µ = 0, 0.31 for µ = 0.4 and 0.37 for µ = 0.8 [26]. Fig. 6 (a) indicates that νmax is a decreasing function of µ (it is not purely geometrical in the quasi-static regime). This shows that the solid fraction, from the critical state to the collisional regime, depends on the frictional properties of the material.
