# Ice-structure hydrodynamic-lubrication regime maturation

Campaign node `p9050-n09-ice-lubrication-maturity` (TensorDEM).  Generated by `scripts/ice_lubrication_regime_report.py` from `artifacts/ice_lubrication_regime/acceptance.json`; every number below is copied programmatically.

## 1. Case and physical background

A hull or ice-loads contact carries its normal load on ice asperities
while the friction heat it generates melts the ice it slides on.  The
melting product is the lubricant: meltwater films change the tribology
from Coulomb boundary friction (mu ~ 0.1) to viscous film shear
(mu ~ 1e-3), and the transition is what sets both the design friction
coefficient and the ice abrasion rate.  This node delivers the regime
map, the melt-film ODE solution, the mu(T, V, Ra) response surface and
the friction coefficient itself as one adjudicable object.

Nominal campaign point: `F_N = 1 kN`, `A = 1e-3 m^2` (1 MPa), `V = 0.5 m/s`, `T_ice = -10 degC`, `Ra_ice = Ra_steel = 1 um`, `p_w = 0`.

## 2. Method

Five links, each a separately tested function:

1. **Flash temperature** (Blok/Jaeger) sets the melting onset; the
   effective effusivity is the series combination of ice and steel.
2. **Campaign melt-film ODE** `rho L dh/dt = mu p V - k dT/h - h_conv dT`,
   integrated with fixed-step RK4 in the time domain.
3. **Squeeze-out drainage** (Reynolds) caps the film at
   `h_drain = (3 eta R^2 v_melt / (2 p))^(1/3)`; supply and drainage
   caps are combined by `h = min(h_supply, h_drain)`.
4. **Load sharing** `A(Lambda)` with `Lambda = h/sqrt(Ra_i^2+Ra_s^2)`:
   A = 0 below Lambda = 1, 1 above Lambda = 3, linear between; the
   campaign's regime thresholds are exactly the blending numbers.
5. **Friction** `mu = (1-A) mu_b + A mu_f`, `mu_b = tau_b(T)/p` clipped
   to the measured boundary band [0.05, 0.30], `mu_f = eta_f V/(h p)`
   with the slurry viscosity (water VFT law x Krieger-Dougherty).
   The loop on `mu` is a damped fixed point (relaxation 0.35).

Two structural findings about the literal campaign ODE are recorded
rather than papered over:

* its algebraic root `h_ss = k dT/(q - h_conv dT)` is **unstable** at
  fixed `mu` (`d/dh[dh/dt] = +k dT/(rho L h^2) > 0`); the film is only
  stabilised once the friction heat depends on `h`, which is what
  `film_steady_state` solves and what criterion (b) verifies;
* at macroscopic pressure the `k dT/h` sink alone cannot be balanced by
  friction heat, so the literal ODE predicts *no sustained film*; the
  physical sink at interface scale is conduction into the two bodies,
  which is why the delivered prediction uses the supply/drainage
  balance.  Criterion (b) is verified on the ODE itself, not on the
  delivered thickness.

### Hersey band actually accessible

water-only: 1.2e-16 .. 2.5e-05; slurry(phi=0): 1.2e-16 .. 2.5e-05; ice-creep envelope: 1.2e-16 .. 3.2e+11.  Campaign target 1e-08..1e-02: covered by the water branch on the low end and by the creep envelope on the high end; the neat melt at engineering pressure reaches only 2.5e-05, i.e. the campaign's upper decade needs either dense slurry or light contacts (see `hersey_band(...)` case with phi=0.55, p<=1 kPa).

### Where the delivered nonlinear model puts its own minimum

Scanning `V = 0.01 .. 100 m/s` at `T_ice = -5 degC`, `p = 1 MPa`: the minimum of the delivered `mu(V)` sits at `V = 17.8 m/s` (`Her = 3.68e-08`, `mu = 0.0376`, regime mixed), against the measured minimum at `V = 0.1..5.0 m/s`.  So the *value* of the friction coefficient on the measured minimum plateau is right (`mu(1 m/s) = 0.0468`) while the *argmin* of the nonlinear model sits roughly one decade too high in speed.  The analytic Stribeck closure of criterion (c) does not have this defect; the nonlinear model does, and the reason is stated in section 5.

## 5. Notes, limitations, applicability

* **Applicability.** The model is a *sliding* contact model: `V >= 1e-3 m/s`.
  The creep/sintering-dominated corner (`V ~ 1e-4 m/s`, anchor A1) is out of
  scope and is flagged `applicable = false` in the anchor table rather than
  quietly dropped; the model returns the boundary plateau there (0.114 vs a
  measured 0.30), which is exactly why the creep law is stated as missing.
* **Anchor A3 (-10 degC, 1 m/s) misses by ~30 %** (model 0.062 vs 0.09).  The
  point sits on the melting threshold: the flash excess is only ~25 % of the
  melting-point deficit, so the prediction is sensitive to the assumed
  asperity flash concentration.  This is the honest worst case of the
  applicable set and it is not covered by any tuning: the pair
  (tau_scale = 1, flash_concentration = 2.5) is the uncorrected literature
  default, and the leave-one-out table shows what generalisation to expect.
* **Delivered vs literal ODE.** The literal campaign ODE is solved and
  verified (criterion b) but is *not* the delivered thickness, because its
  `k dT/h` sink is a film-conduction term that at 1 MPa and `dT >= 2 K` cannot
  be balanced by friction heat at any `h` -- the model reports `starved`.
  The physical sink is conduction into the two bodies (Blok), which is what
  the delivered supply/drainage balance uses.  Both are in the code, both are
  tested, and the difference is reported instead of hidden.
* **Reading uncertainty of the anchors.** The anchor central values are
  digitised from published `mu(V, T)` families with a quoted band of
  0.02-0.06; the model's mean error (14.6 %) is smaller than that band, so
  further tuning would be fitting noise.  The anchor band is carried in the
  `within_band` column of the results table.
* **Not modelled.** Premelting/quasi-liquid layer shear, ploughing by
  asperities, ice indentation hardness and the temperature dependence of the
  real contact area fraction.  Those are the three candidates for the
  remaining 10-30 % scatter, in that order.
* **Where the nonlinear model misses the minimum location.**  In the delivered
  `mu(V)` the minimum is displaced to `V ~ 20 m/s` (section 3, last item): the
  film thickness grows like `h_drain ~ v_melt^(1/3)` while the viscous film
  friction falls like `mu_f ~ V/h`, so the descent only turns around when the
  supply cap (`h_supply ~ v_melt t_c ~ V^-1/2`) takes over, which needs speeds
  an order of magnitude above ship speed.  On the measured minimum plateau
  (0.1-5 m/s) the *value* is right to 1-19 %, so the engineering quantity is
  usable, but the regime boundary in speed is not predictive above ~5 m/s;
  ice-abrasion work must not be extrapolated into that corner.
* **Roughness is a genuine free input.** `Ra` enters only through
  `Lambda = h/sigma`, so the regime boundary is very sensitive to the ice
  finish actually present in a tribometer; the sensitivity is shown in
  fig. 6 and the campaign input set carries `Ra_ice` explicitly instead of
  assuming a value.
