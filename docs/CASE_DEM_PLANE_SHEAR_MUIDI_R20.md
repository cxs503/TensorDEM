## ① Case and protocol

A plane-shear cell of 1276 spheres (d = 10 mm, rho = 2500 kg/m³) is sheared between a servo-controlled platen and a frictional floor in a box 0.16×0.08×0.09 m (32d×16d×18d).  The platen holds a constant normal stress P = 62.5 kPa by a servo loop and drives a shear rate chosen to reach the target inertial number.  Contact law: linear spring-dashpot, kn = 62500 N/m (P/kn = 1), kt/kn = 0.5, mu_contact = 0.5, mu_rolling = 0.05, e = 0.3, seed 5279.

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| I targets | 0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3 |
| box (W×D×H) | 0.16 × 0.08 × 0.09 m |
| particles | 1276 (N > 1000; rounds 1-16 used 294) |
| normal stress P | 62.50 kPa (servo) |
| kn / kt | 62500 / 31250 N/m |
| contact / rolling friction | 0.5 / 0.05 |
| restitution | 0.3 |
| settle steps | 3000 |
| shear strain target | 0.5 |
| dt | 3.21e-05 s |

Two gaps are closed here, both of them *coverage* gaps rather than new physics.

1. **Range.** Rounds 18/19 ran the higher-N study only at I ≥ 1e-2; the ledger acceptance starts at 1e-3.  The low-I half was measured only at N = 294, where rounds 14/16 showed the noise floor exceeds the 10 % band.  This round measures the whole ledger range at N > 1000.
2. **Verdict unit.** Round 13 fixed the reference side (two conflicting triples → an admissible envelope) on the *N = 294* archive with the imposed-rate abscissa; round 19 fixed the abscissa (measured bulk rate) but imported a single triple and quoted a single mu estimator.  Here the verdict is reported as a matrix — {3 mu estimators} × {3 abscissae} × {2 candidate triples + the falsification oracle} — so the ledger cannot be failed or passed by an unreported estimator choice.

Pre-registered questions (`PREREG` in the module, written before submission):
* **Q1_range** — every ledger-range point is measured at N>1000, so the verdict no longer rests on N=294 low-I points
* **Q2_estimator** — the estimator spread (wall / full-cell / bulk; p vs sigma_zz) is smaller than the 10% band, i.e. the verdict does not depend on which one is quoted
* **Q3_abscissa** — the measured-rate abscissa moves mu inside the band where the imposed-rate one does not (round-19 claim)
* **Q4_falsification** — best_case_over_envelope answers whether ANY admissible triple can hold the curve inside 10%; `provenance_can_rescue=False` means the residual is structural, not bibliographic
* **Q5_honest** — the worst-point error of the best arm is reported whether or not it passes

## ② Method

* Device: `tensordem.mu_i_rheology.simulate_servo_shear` (unchanged since round 17); protocol parity with rounds 18/19 (same contact law, P/kn, seed, strain target, box), so the four shared I targets double as a reproducibility check against the round-19 sweep.
* `high_n_coverage.summarise()` is pure and records, per point: three mu estimators (wall `tau/p` at the platen, full-cell `tau/<p>`, bulk-slab `tau/p`), both abscissae (imposed platen rate, measured velocity-profile rate) plus the round-19 fallback, three phi estimators, the noise floor of the final 25 % window, the pressure-control error, the rate ratio and the servo gate.
* `assemble()` builds the verdict matrix, calls `reference_provenance.verdict_table` (per-triple pass/fail) and `best_case_over_envelope` (falsification oracle over the continuous box mu_s ∈ [0.34,0.40], mu_2 ∈ [0.55,0.95], I_0 ∈ [0.22,0.38]).  A triple whose fit sits on a box edge is reported as *not identified* rather than quoted as a calibration.
* Reference constants are imported from `reference_law` (single source of truth, round-19 defect D-A) and every residual is a percentage compared against a percentage band (round-19 defect D-C).

## ⑤ Notes, limits, and what this round did **not** do

* **Shear strain.** The sweep stops at strain 0.5, a deliberate compromise for the higher-N cost (round 16 found ≥ 2 for full stationarity at low I).  Low-I points are therefore the least converged rows of Table 1; the direction of the bias is discussed in round 16 and not re-derived here.
* **Reference provenance is unchanged.** The two conflicting triples (0.36/0.84/0.30 in `reference_law`, 0.379/0.641/0.279 in the test suite) are both still only *quoted*; this round does not resolve that, it makes the verdict independent of the choice (envelope + oracle).
* **phi is not an acceptance.** The magnitudes (0.58, 0.64) have no in-repo literature anchor and da Cruz 2005 is a 2D-disk study (phi ≈ 0.84→0.79); only the monotonicity/limits are verified.  Reported as WITHHELD, as in rounds 13-19.
* **Verifier findings fixed in this round** (found by re-reading the shipped harness, both now pinned by tests):
  * `high_n_sweep.assemble` reported `P1_noise_floor`/`P2_mu_convergence` as `True` when *no* mid-I point was present — `all([])` is vacuously true, so a dropped shard would have printed a PASS on the two hypotheses that carry the round-18 prediction.  Now gated on `mid_points_present`.
  * this round's own `mu_passes_full_range` would have accepted a band-pass over a *partial* range; it is now gated on `range_complete`.
* Cluster job semantics: results are for job `cj3315`; the four I targets shared with round 18/19 are an independent reproduction at the same seed (identical protocol), not a second measurement of a different ensemble.

## ⑦ Hand-off state (honest)

The sweep is a cluster job (`cj3315`, 6 shards, one I target per shard) submitted
from this worktree.  At hand-off it had run 50 min and had written **one** of the
six points (`I = 3e-3`, 210 s of device time, N = 1276); the queue meanwhile
carried 30-47 jobs in flight, so the shards are being served slowly.  The round-18
higher-N job `cj3277` (submitted 111 min earlier by round 19, same protocol) had
also produced only one point (`I = 3e-2`).  **No mu(I)/phi(I) curve verdict is
claimed here**; everything in ③/④/⑥ above is generated from the single landed
point and is marked `range_complete=False` by construction.

What the single landed point already shows, and why it matters for the protocol:

| quantity | value at I_target = 3e-3 |
|---|---|
| mu_wall (tau/p at platen) | 0.2299 |
| mu_full (tau/<p>) | 0.4355 |
| mu_bulk (bulk slab) | 0.3949 |
| estimator spread (wall vs full) | **89 %** |
| mu noise floor (final 25 % window) | **14.0 %** (> 10 % band) |
| measured / imposed shear-rate ratio q | **-0.010** |
| I from measured profile rate | 9.5e-5 (imposed 2.97e-3) |
| p/sigma_zz | 0.554 |
| phi (bed / extent / container) | 0.5923 / 0.5869 / 0.5921 |

The bed is **not shearing** at this point (`q ~ 0`, measured rate 30x below the
imposed rate) while the servo holds the normal stress with `p/sigma_zz = 0.55`,
i.e. the cell is still in its start-up transient at strain 0.5.  Consequences:

* the three mu estimators disagree by 89 %, far outside the 10 % band — so at low
  I the *estimator choice alone* can flip a ledger verdict (wall: -36 %, bulk:
  +9.6 % against the same reference point);
* the "measured-rate" abscissa of round 19 is **degenerate** where the profile
  fit collapses (q ~ 0): it reports I = 9.5e-5 instead of 3e-3, i.e. a different
  material state rather than a better abscissa;
* the noise floor (14 %) exceeds the acceptance band, reproducing the round-14/16
  conclusion that low-I points need a longer strain window, not a bigger box.

So the remaining work for this anchor is a **strain-window** study at low I
(strain >= 1-2 per round 16) rather than more particles: 1e-3/3e-3 at strain 0.5
are transient rows and must not be quoted as steady-state rheology.
