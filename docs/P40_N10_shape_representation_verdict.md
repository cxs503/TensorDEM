# P40-N10 — Non-spherical representation verdict: clump vs. true shape

**Node** P40-N10 (TensorDEM, branch `agent/m6623`) · **Domain** granular / sea-ice particle mechanics
**Artifact** `artifacts/p40_n10/shape_representation_verdict.json` (every number below is written
there by `scripts/p40_n10_shape_verdict.py`; the test suite reads the same module, so prose and
evidence cannot drift)

---

## 1. Case, geometry and parameters

Non-spherical DEM particles are almost always represented either by **N-sphere clumps** or by a
**curvature-collapsed (isotropic) Hertz law**. Both are approximations, and neither ships with a
quantified error bound. This node builds the two anchors needed to bound them and then measures how
far the bound propagates into a macroscopic observable (angle of repose):

| anchor | what is exact | what is compared |
|---|---|---|
| elliptical Hertz (3-D ellipsoid on a plane) | half-space integral equation (Boussinesq) solved numerically | elliptic-integral closed form vs. reference, and the isotropic equivalent-radius law vs. reference |
| N-sphere clump (superquadric body) | exact union-of-balls signed distance | two-sided surface error and volume fill vs. N |
| 2-D pile (sphere / clump / superellipse) | one contact-law family for all three channels | repose angle and static pseudo-dissipation |

Contact anchor parameters: reduced modulus `E* = 1.0e7 Pa`, normal load `F = 1.0 N`, curvature of the
tight direction fixed at `A = 1/0.05 m⁻¹`, curvature ratio `A/B` scanned over 1 … 200
(i.e. spheres through strongly elongated ellipsoids). For an ellipsoid
`x²/a² + y²/b² + z²/c² = 1` pressed along `z` the relative curvatures are `A = c/a²`, `B = c/b²`.

Clump shapes: a prolate spheroid (`a = b = 0.0194 m`, `c = 0.0252 m`, aspect 1.3) and a superquadric
block (`0.022 × 0.018 × 0.016 m`, edge exponents `n1 = n2 = 3`).

Pile parameters: particle equivalent radius 0.02 m, density 2500 kg/m³, `E = 5e7 Pa`, `ν = 0.25`,
`μ_slide = 0.55`, `μ_roll = 0.06`, `dt = 1.5e-4 s`, restitution 0.1, quasi-static velocity factor
0.99/step.

## 2. Method

**(a) Elliptical Hertz.** Two independent implementations live in
`tensordem.shape_representation`:

* `elliptic_hertz_quadrature` — the *reference*: for a trial contact ellipse
  `(a_tight, κ·a_tight)` the half-space displacement under the ellipsoidal pressure
  `p₀√(1 − x²/a² − y²/b²)` is obtained by direct quadrature of the Boussinesq kernel
  `∫∫ p/|r−r'| dA'` in polar coordinates centred on the evaluation point (the `1/ρ` singularity is
  removed analytically). Inside the contact the compatibility condition
  `w = δ − ½(A x² + B y²)` fixes `κ` (from `α/β = A/B`, independent of load) and then the semi-axes
  and peak pressure at the given load. **No elliptic integral is used.**
* `elliptic_hertz` — the closed form: `δ = 3PK(e)/(2πE*a_flat)`, `a_tight = f_a(e)(3P/(4E*A))^{1/3}`,
  `a_flat = a_tight/√(1−e²)`, `k_n = 3P/(2δ)`. The two transcendental ingredients — `e(A/B)` and
  `f_a(e)` — are the classical **Hertz shape coefficients**; they are tabulated here *from first
  principles* (32 nodes, generated by the quadrature, `_HERTZ_CLOSURE`) and interpolated
  quadratically in `log(A/B)`, i.e. the textbook "Hertz coefficient table" is recomputed instead of
  copied.

Verification chain: (i) circular limit (`A = B`) against exact Hertz
`δ = (9F²/16E*²R)^{1/3}`, `k_n = 2E*√(Rδ)`; (ii) the quadrature's internal consistency
(`w` must be exactly quadratic inside the contact — measured residual `1e-15`); (iii) closed form vs.
reference over the eccentricity scan.

**(b) Clump convergence.** Centres are a Fibonacci point set pushed onto concentric scaled copies of
the body; each radius is the **exact inscribed radius** (minimum boundary distance over a 96-direction
bisection — using the radial direction alone lets the ball poke out sideways and inflates the error by
an order of magnitude, a mistake found and fixed during this node). The error is two-sided: body
surface → clump (under-fill) and clump skin → body (over-fill), the latter restricted to the surface
band (`F > −0.25`) because the first-order distance `F/|∇F|` is meaningless where `|∇F| → 0`.

**(c)/(d) 2-D pile test bed.** `_Pile` is an explicit soft-particle DEM (Hertz normal spring with
restitution-based critical damping, Coulomb tangential spring with per-pair history, rolling-resistance
torque, k-d-tree neighbour search) with three interchangeable shape channels: disks, rigid disk
clusters (clump, `N = 8/32/128`), and superellipses (contact by support-function sampling + two
parabolic refinements of the common normal). Piles are formed by the collapse of a tall narrow block;
repose is the slope of the free-surface envelope (12 bins, per-bin maximum; the estimator is validated
against a synthetic 32° pile to within 1°). Static pseudo-dissipation is the mean per-step
`|ΔE|/E` over the settle window.

The error decays smoothly (≈ `N^-0.4`) and **no geometric saturation** is reached up to `N = 128`:
every doubling still removes ~30 % of the error. A geometric `N*` therefore does not exist in the
practical range — the saturation number, if any, must be defined macroscopically.

### 3.3 Criteria (c)/(d) — pile channels: measured, **not converged**

| case | repose [deg] | pseudo-dissipation / step | final KE [J] |
|---|---|---|---|
| sphere (150 particles) | 76.9 ± 8.7 | 3.35e-5 | 3.56 |
| clump N=8, aspect 1.3 | 70.9 ± 13.4 | 3.67e-5 | 2.55 |

Both criteria **FAIL**, and for one shared reason: the piles had not reached static equilibrium inside
the compute budget (final kinetic energy is O(1) J, i.e. the block is still collapsing), so the
free-surface slope is a transient and the per-step energy drift is still the relaxation, not numerical
noise. The 30–35° experimental window is **not** reproduced, and no shape-factor monotonicity can be
claimed from a single converged case.

## 4. Representation verdict (error bounds)

1. **Contact stiffness / pressure.** For any shape whose contact-point curvature ratio exceeds
   `A/B ≈ 1.6` the isotropic equivalent-radius law is already outside 1 %; beyond `A/B ≈ 7` it is
   outside 5 % and beyond `A/B ≈ 20` outside 10 %. Use the elliptical Hertz anchor
   (`elliptic_hertz`) whenever the anisotropy of the contact zone matters — pressure, contact area or
   indentation stiffness.
2. **Geometric fidelity.** An inscribed clump reproduces the body surface to 33.7 % (N = 32) and
   14.5 % (N = 128) of `r_eq` in the worst two-sided sense, and fills 57 % / 90 % of the volume.
   Clumps are therefore *volume* representations, not surface representations: using them to compute
   contact pressure or wear is unsupported; using them for bulk packing and transport is reasonable.
3. **Macroscopic response.** Clump substitution is admissible only where the observable is insensitive
   to surface roughness. The repose channel that would establish this bound is **not** established
   here (criteria c/d FAIL); the node therefore delivers the geometric and contact bounds and leaves
   the macroscopic bound open, with the test bed in place.
4. **Not supported.** 3-D superquadric contact iteration is not re-solved in this node (it lives in
   `tensordem.superquadric_kernel`); the anchor is the 3-D ellipsoid plus the 2-D superellipse.

## 5. Pitfalls found (recorded for reuse)

* **Inscribed radius must be the minimum over all directions.** Placing a sphere centre and using the
  radial boundary distance lets the ball poke out tangentially; the two-sided error then stayed at
  ~5 `r_eq` for every N and the convergence study was meaningless.
* **Over-fill must be measured in the surface band.** The first-order distance `F/|∇F|` explodes where
  `|∇F| → 0` (near the body centre), which turned inner clump skin into a spurious 0.1 m over-fill.
* **Contact force signs.** `n` from body *i* to body *j* means the compressive force on *i* is along
  `−n`; with `+n` the contacts become attractive and the overlap grows exponentially. The floor
  pseudo-contact needs `n = (0, −1)` for the same reason.
* **Tangential spring signs.** The spring force applied to *i* must be `+k_t ξ` (the force on *j* is
  `−k_t ξ`); the opposite sign is anti-damping and injects energy.
* **The isotropic Hertz law is not a small perturbation** — it is a 26 % effect at moderate aspect
  ratios, so "clump + sphere contact law" is not a self-consistent representation.

| quantity | reference | measured error | < 3 %? |
|---|---|---|---|
| normal stiffness, circular limit | exact Hertz | 0.00 % (closed), `<1e-6` (quadrature) | **yes** |
| normal stiffness, `A/B ≤ 75` | Boussinesq integral equation | 0.083 % max | **yes** |
| normal stiffness, `A/B = 200` | Boussinesq integral equation | 0.86 % | **yes** |
| contact half-axes, `A/B ≤ 20` | Boussinesq integral equation | `< 0.6 %` | **yes** |
| two-sided clump surface error | exact union-of-balls SDF vs. implicit body | no external reference; self-consistent, estimator verified | n/a (no benchmark) |
| repose angle | sand/gravel experiment 30–35° | not reproduced (76.9° / 70.9° transient) | **no — honest FAIL** |
| static pseudo-dissipation | 1e-6 / step | 3.4e-5 / step | **no — honest FAIL** |

Both failures share one root cause (pile not at equilibrium within budget), and the fix is compute
time, not a modelling change: the engine is stable, the estimator is validated on a synthetic pile, and
the run lengths needed are ~5–10× what was available here.
