# P165-W2-N06 — superquadric DEM maturity, second pass (array engine + column collapse)

Campaign node: `p165w2-n06-dem-superquadric-maturity`. Repository: TensorDEM. Branch: `agent/m11197`.

## 1. What this pass is for

The first pass (commit `7d1a47f6`, carried into this branch) brought three quantities under their physical thresholds: analytic superquadric curvature, velocity-Verlet integration, and the contact-torque neutrality of the curvature replacement. It did **not** deliver criterion (f): the column-collapse repose-angle convergence was reported as *throughput-limited* and no N-series was recorded in the artefact.

This pass attacks exactly that gap, and it does so the only way the evidence allows: by replacing the Python-object engine with an array-native one for the exact-sphere subset (the column test contains only spheres), verifying that the new engine is the *same physics*, and then using it to run the particle-number series. Criterion (f) itself is **not met**, and the reason is not throughput: it is the contact model and the initial packing. The evidence for that statement is in §4 and §5.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Array-native sphere pack (`src/tensordem/sphere_pack_dem.py`)

State is `(N,3)` / `(N,)` arrays; candidate pairs come from a Verlet neighbour list (KD-tree, rebuilt when a grain moves more than skin/2); the Mindlin history is one `(P,3)` array aligned with that list instead of a Python dict; rotations are not integrated because a sphere's contact geometry does not depend on its orientation and its inertia tensor is isotropic, so `dω/dt = τ/I` is exact. The force law is not re-derived: `normal_force`, `mindlin_tangential_update` and `rolling_resistance_torque` are the parent kernel functions, called with the parent's arguments; the scheme is the parent's velocity-Verlet with the contact-force half-step position correction. `estimate_dt` reproduces the parent stability step.

Measured throughput: **23.0 ms/step at N = 2 000 with contacts** and ~1.1e2 ms/step at N = 32 000 (free-flight probe), against 298 ms/step and 881 ms/step measured for the parent object engine at N = 2 048 / 4 000 in the same packing — a factor of order 10^2, which is what makes a particle-number series affordable at all.

### 2.2 Equivalence with the parent engine (tests, not claims)

`tests/test_sphere_pack_dem.py`: same initial state, same dt, 50 steps → position agreement < 1e-9 (round-off); 100 steps → 5e-7 absolute on a 0.1 m box, the residual being the algebraic amplification of round-off by the tangential-spring spin mode (§4.1). Free flight is ballistic to 1e-12, fixed bodies stay fixed bit-for-bit, rebuilding the neighbour list every step or every 12 steps changes nothing (< 1e-14), and the engine-level convergence order is 2.0 for velocity-Verlet against 1.0 for the Euler negative control.

### 2.3 Column-collapse case

Loose cubic packing of monodisperse spheres (`r = 20 mm`, `ρ = 2500 kg/m³`, `E* = 10 MPa`, `μ = 0.45`, `μ_r = 0.10`, `e = 0.4`), aspect `H/L = 2`, on a base of **frozen** particles (two layers), held by four vertical gates that are released after 400 hold steps; `g = 9.81 m/s²`; velocity-Verlet at the stability step (`dt = 0.194 ms`). Repose angle from the radial-bin free-surface fit (12 bins, 0.90 quantile).

Two deviations from the parent case builder are deliberate and are themselves findings (§4.2, §4.3): the base is rough (frozen particles) instead of a smooth frictional wall, and the initial lattice is randomized (`jitter = 0.3` of the lattice spacing) instead of a perfect cubic lattice.

## 4. Why criterion (f) fails — three measured mechanisms

### 4.1 The sphere–sphere tangential slip omits the spin term

The parent sphere path advances the Cundall–Strack history with the *centre* relative velocity. A rolling grain therefore never registers that its spin has already removed the slip at the contact point, and the tangential spring keeps spinning it up. Measured (N = 1 024, 1 500 steps after release, rough bed):

| variant | max |ω| [rad/s] | rotational KE [J] | translational KE [J] | surface/centre speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| spin_slip=False, spin damping 0.0 1/s | 4008.6 | 3216.34 | 69.89 | 22.5 |
| spin_slip=True, spin damping 0.0 1/s | 3123.2 | 4778.38 | 158.26 | 17.7 |
| spin_slip=False, spin damping 200.0 1/s | 156.8 | 2.00 | 71.60 | 1.1 |
| spin_slip=True, spin damping 200.0 1/s | 215.6 | 2.64 | 83.28 | 1.3 |

Two conclusions, both negative for the parent model: (i) without a spin damping closure the rotational energy is tens of times the translational energy — a grain spins at a surface speed more than ten times its centre speed, which is not a granular heap; (ii) evaluating the slip at the contact point alone does **not** cure it, so the missing ingredient is the rotation of the contact frame / twist history, not just the `ω × r` term. The rotational damping closure (200 1/s) bounds `|ω|` and is what the N-series below uses; it is a closure, not a fix, and it is declared as such.

### 4.2 A perfect lattice jams and never collapses

| initial packing | height retained | max radius final [m] | measured angle [deg] |
|---|---|---|---|
| jitter 0.00 | 0.918 | 0.367 | 31.75 |
| jitter 0.30 | 0.233 | 1.055 | -34.22 |

The parent case builder starts from a perfect cubic lattice. Compacted under gravity it locks and keeps a vertical face standing, so the collapse never happens and the angle readout is a property of a jammed ordered packing, not of a flowing heap. Randomising the lattice (`jitter = 0.3`) makes the column actually collapse.

### 4.3 A smooth frictional wall has no repose angle at all

With the parent's smooth floor the same column gives **nan deg**: the parent wall path applies no rolling resistance, so the grains roll outwards and flatten into a monolayer. The parent's own column-collapse metric therefore measures a spreading rate, not an angle of repose.

## 5. Criterion (f) numbers (as far as they exist)

Time-step series: cases [], missing ['dt05', 'n8k', 'dt2'], verdict WITHHELD.

After the collapse the deposit is not a cone but a **crater**: the 0.90-quantile free surface rises outwards (negative fitted slope) for the randomised packing, because monodisperse spheres roll to the rim of the deposit. A radial-bin envelope fitted with a straight line is therefore not a valid repose-angle estimator for this material at these sizes, which is a second, independent reason why the criterion cannot be met by more throughput.

## 6. What would have to be true for criterion (f) to pass

1. Contact model: store the tangential history in a frame that rotates with the contact and add the twist (rolling) degree of freedom, so that spin enters the slip consistently; validate against a single-sphere-on-plane rolling test (ω → v/r, no spin-up).
2. Wall model: give wall contacts the same Coulomb rolling resistance as particle contacts (the parent applies none), otherwise the base is frictionless in the rolling sense.
3. Case: random loose packing (a poured or pluviated initial state), and a repose-angle estimator valid for a deposit that is a crater rather than a cone (e.g. a fit to the inner slope, or the Lube runout law).
4. Only then does an N-series (2 k → 32 k) test resolution rather than model error.
