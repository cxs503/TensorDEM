# Pressure–area index from DEM indentation: non-simultaneous failure as the cause

Generated by `scripts/pressure_area_report.py` from `artifacts/pressure_area_index/summary.json`; every number below is injected programmatically.

## 1. Question and physical background

The global ice pressure transmitted by a wide structure face is observed to fall with the nominal contact area, `p_G = C A^-ex` with `ex ~ 0.5` (Sanderson 1988; Masterson & Frederking; Dempsey). Two candidate mechanisms are quoted: (i) the true contact area is a growing fraction of the nominal area, and (ii) ice strength is spatially variable, so failure is **non-simultaneous** and the peak load is set by progressive collapse, not by one brittle event. This study makes both mechanisms *computed* inside a bonded-particle DEM instead of assumed, and uses a perfectly correlated strength field (simultaneous failure) as the negative control.

## 2. Model (module `src/tensordem/pressure_area_dem.py`)

* Rigid punch of width `w` driven quasi-statically into a triangular bonded-particle ice layer of thickness `h = 0.2 m`, particle diameter `2r = 0.04 m`, plane-strain 2-D.
* Nominal contact area `A = w x 1 m`; `E = 9.00e+09 Pa`, `nu = 0.333`; lattice calibration `E_2D = 2k/sqrt(3)` (verified in the test suite to 5 %).
* Contact law: Winkler foundation with the geometry-consistent Hertz width `b = sqrt(2 r delta)`, `F = 2 K_c b delta ~ delta^1.5`, so the true contact area `A_c = sum 2 b` grows as `sqrt(delta)` — **mechanism (i)**.
* Strength field: spatially correlated lognormal, median `0.001`, `COV = 0.52` (Weibull-modulus equivalent `m ~ 2`), correlation length `l = 0.1 m`; a single field drives both bond failure strains and contact crushing — **mechanism (ii)**.
* Punch-face roughness `sigma = 0.02 r` (lateral correlation 0.08 m) supplies the load non-uniformity that makes contact elements fail one by one instead of all at once.
* Equilibrium: Newton iterations on the SPD system `(K + diag(dF/ddelta)) du = q(u) - K u`; bonds fail irreversibly; particles that lose their bond path to the clamped base leave the contact. Reported `p_G` is the pressure at the **first brittle failure** (elastic limit).

### 3.2 Exponent versus strength statistics

| scan | COV | equivalent Weibull m | ex | ex x m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| primary | 0.52 | 2.04 | 0.307 | 0.627 |
| m_sensitivity_cov0.36 | 0.36 | 2.86 | 0.216 | 0.618 |

The product `ex x m = 0.617-0.627` is constant across a factor 1.9 in the equivalent Weibull modulus, i.e. the DEM obeys `ex = (0.62 +- 0.01) / m` -- the classical weakest-link scaling with a geometry factor 0.62 instead of 1.  Reproducing the field value ex = 0.5 would then require m ~ 1.2, which is outside the ice literature range (m = 2-5): in this 2-D punched-layer geometry the strength statistics alone cannot produce ex = 0.5.

### 3.3 Contact-area growth (mechanism i)

Shortest punch (`A = 0.5 m^2`): the true contact area rises from 0.0040 m to 0.2435 m per metre depth while the punch advances 0.100 -> 2.800 mm, i.e. the contact fraction is a computed, growing quantity; the `A_c ~ u^0.5` law is asserted in the test suite.

## 5. Verdict against the acceptance criteria

* `ex` in [0.45, 0.55]: **FAIL** (measured 0.307).
* negative control `ex -> 0`: **PASS** (measured 0.099).
* ex(finite l) > ex(l = inf) with disjoint CIs: **PASS**.

## 6. Limitations

* Plane-strain 2-D lattice: absolute pressures are 2-D values; the exponent is the transferable quantity.
* The measured exponent is systematically below the classical weakest-link value `1/m`: the clamped base bounds the stressed volume by the layer thickness, so the effective number of independent strength patches grows slower than the nominal area.
* `p_G` is the elastic-limit (first-failure) pressure; post-yield comminution and steady-state crushing are out of scope (the `contact_crushing` switch exists but is off).
* Lattice resolution and contact stiffness are numerical parameters; their sensitivity is tabulated in section 3.4 and must be read before quoting the second decimal of `ex`.
