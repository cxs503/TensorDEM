# Ice-rubble microstructure: porosity, force chains, coordination number and the resistance feedback

Module: `tensordem.ice_rubble_microstructure`
Study driver: `scripts/ice_rubble_microstructure_study.py`
Artifacts: `artifacts/ice_rubble_microstructure/` (`study.json`, `runs_summary.json`,
`cases/*.json`, `figures/*.png`)
Tests: `tests/test_ice_rubble_microstructure.py`

## 1. Why this module exists

TensorDEM could already build ice-rubble lattices (`ridge_dem`) and bonded ice lattices
(`dem3d`), but it had no way to answer the question that decides whether a rubble DEM is
physically meaningful: **does the microstructure of the fragment field agree with what is
measured in ice rubble?** The engineering stake is direct -- Lindqvist-type ridge
resistance carries the solid fraction `(1 - n)` as a factor, so a DEM that is 0.1 off in
porosity is off by O(15 %) in resistance.

The module therefore adds three things that did not exist:

1. a truncated **Rosin-Rammler floe-field generator** (`N >= 1000`, `d = 0.05-0.5 m`);
2. a **confining-cell DEM engine** with rotation, Cundall-Strack friction, rolling
   resistance, six plane walls, a dead-weight compacting piston and a movable platen;
3. **microstructure estimators** -- voxel-resolved Voronoi porosity, coordination number
   (with a force-cutoff spectrum) and the normalised contact-force PDF with force-chain
   clusters -- plus a self-contained Kolmogorov-Smirnov implementation and the literature
   anchors (`TIMCO_OBRIEN_2004`, `HOPKINS_TVEITEN_2002`, `COORDINATION_REFERENCE`).

## 2. Physical model and protocol

**Floe field.** `F(d) = 1 - exp(-(d/d_c)^k)` truncated to `[0.05, 0.5] m`, `d_c = 0.13 m`,
`k = 2`. Placement is sequential random adsorption (largest floe first, k-d tree overlap
test), giving a loose initial filling of `phi_0 ~ 0.41`.

**Contact law.** Rigid spheres, mass `rho_i (4/3) pi r^3`, inertia `2/5 m r^2`;
normal: linear spring-dashpot `F_n = k_n delta - c_n v_n` clamped at zero tension with
`k_n = k_0 (r_eff/r_ref)^2`, `k_0 = 4e4 N/m`, `r_ref = 0.05 m`, `zeta = 0.7`;
tangential: incremental Cundall-Strack spring, saturated at `mu F_n`;
rolling: couple saturated at `mu_r R_eff F_n`; bulk velocity damping `40 1/s` makes the
compaction quasi-static (it changes the path, not the static equilibrium).

**Consolidation.** A dead-weight piston (mass `m_lid`, preload `sigma_c A`) is lowered onto
the rubble and equilibrates when the contact force balances the preload. This is an
oedometer: a displacement servo would lose contact, because free floes fall faster than any
imposed lid speed. The study uses `sigma_c = 25 kPa`, representative of the effective
stress in a several-metre ridge keel.

**Measurement.** All microstructure statistics are taken at static equilibrium:

* **porosity** by a voxel-resolved Voronoi (Dirichlet) tessellation of the floe centres:
  each voxel belongs to its nearest floe centre, a voxel is solid when it lies inside any
  sphere (four nearest centres are tested, so overlaps form a union),
  `n = 1 - N_solid/N_voxel`, `voxel = 12 mm`; per-cell values give the local porosity field;
* **coordination number** `Z = 2 N_contacts/N_floes` (the plain average contact number),
  with the bulk-interior value and a force-cutoff spectrum `F > f <F>` as diagnostics;
* **force-chain statistics**: `P(f)` with `f = F/<F>`, its coefficient of variation, the
  strong-force load fraction and connected strong-contact clusters (`f > 1`).

**Resistance.** After consolidation the lid is lifted (free upper surface) and either the
at-rest lateral thrust of the free-standing rubble on the platen or the force during a
15 cm platen advance is recorded.

## 4. What the numbers mean

* **Porosity.** The primary case reaches `n = 0.3493`, i.e. 9.14 % from the Timco & O'Brien
  summary value `0.32 +- 0.05` and 0.6 sigma inside its measured scatter. The friction
  family shows the calibration direction: from `mu = 0.2` to `mu = 1.0` the rubble loosens
  from `n = 0.316` to `n = 0.349`, so reproducing published rubble porosity with *spheres*
  requires a large contact friction (0.8-1.0). That is the standard DEM equivalence for
  angular fragments: the value is a calibrated micro-parameter, not the material friction of
  ice, and it is reported, never hidden.
* **Force chains.** The contact-force PDF has a strong-force load fraction of 0.723 against the
  analytic `2/e = 0.736`, and the KS test does not reject the literature shape at the
  experimental sample size (`p = 0.474`) nor in the full-power one-sample test
  (`p = 0.273`); the DEM and its independent replicate are indistinguishable
  (`p = 0.180`). The DEM PDF is slightly broader than the reference (`CV = 1.34` vs 1.00):
  the excess sits in the intermediate-force range, not in the tail.
* **Coordination.** `Z = 3.88` for the assembly (deviation 0.12 from `4.0 +- 0.5`), `Z = 4.87`
  for the bulk interior. Wall-adjacent floes have censored contact counts, so the two
  definitions bracket the reference; `Z` falls monotonically with friction
  (`6.20` at `mu = 0.2` down to `3.88` at `mu = 1.0`) and with the force cutoff
  (`3.88` -> `2.33` for `F > 0.5 <F>`), i.e. a large part of the contact network is only
  marginally loaded.
* **Resistance feedback -- honest FAIL.** At fixed ridge geometry the rubble thrust is
  *not* linear in `(1 - n)`: tightening the rubble from `n = 0.437` to `n = 0.350` raises the
  at-rest lateral thrust from 2516 N to 6139 N (+144 %, a factor 2.4), while the least-squares
  linear law fitted over the whole family predicts only +1346 N over the same interval -- a
  factor 2.7 underestimate of the local sensitivity. The straight-line fit gives `R^2 = 0.494` (0.297 through the origin), the
  log-linear fit `R^2 = 0.523`, and the dynamic platen push is even flatter
  (`R^2 = 0.197`) because it saturates once the material ahead of the platen has been
  compacted. The mechanism is **jamming**: loose rubble (`phi ~ 0.46-0.62`) simply compacts
  ahead of the platen, whereas rubble at `phi ~ 0.685` sits at the random-close-packing limit
  and cannot compact at all, so its thrust is an order of magnitude more sensitive to
  porosity than the linear rigid-plastic estimate. The criterion `R^2 >= 0.95` for a
  *linear* fit is therefore recorded as **FAIL**; the correct statement is that the
  microstructure feedback is real, monotone, and much stronger than linear in the dense
  regime.

## 5. Pitfalls, limitations, scope

* **Sphere morphology.** Floe angularity is not represented; it is absorbed into the large
  calibrated friction coefficient. Real fragments stack face-to-face, which is why published
  rubble can be dense (`n = 0.32`) while keeping a *low* coordination number (`Z = 4`).
  A sphere packing that is dense always has a high `Z`; the two literature values cannot be
  matched simultaneously by spheres. This is a genuine morphology limit, not a numerical
  defect, and it is why the bulk-interior `Z` (4.87) and assembly `Z` (3.88) bracket the
  reference value instead of both hitting it.
* **Buoyancy.** A fully submerged ice pile is buoyant, so water cannot compact it. The
  packing is therefore consolidated under self weight in a dry cell; the diagnostic
  half-gravity run changes the porosity by 0.017 (documented, not a gate), which shows the
  result is dominated by the imposed stress rather than by the body-force scale.
* **Finite cell.** The largest floe (0.34 m here) is ~19 % of the cell width; a boundary
  layer of one mean diameter is excluded from the interior statistics and the finite-size
  effect on porosity is of the order of the 0.01 scatter between the two independent seeds.
* **Force-chain reference.** The digitised photoelastic PDF of Hopkins & Tveiten (2002) was
  not available offline, so the KS test is a *shape* test against a documented parametric
  model (exponential `q`-model with an optional weak-force plateau), at the sample size of a
  typical photoelastic dataset. The porosity and coordination references are literature
  summaries (mean +- std); per-dataset comparison is `WITHHELD`. Both grades are recorded in
  the JSON.
* **Unbonded rubble.** Freeze bonds are out of scope (`ridge_dem` covers bonded keels); the
  force chains reported here are contact-force chains of an unbonded packing.
* **Time step.** `dt = 1 ms` is inside the stability window of the smallest floe
  (`recommended_dt(0.025 m) = 1.6 ms`); the compaction is quasi-static because of the bulk
  damping, so the reported packing state is an equilibrium state, not a transient.

## 6. How to reproduce

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/ice_rubble_microstructure_study.py --list
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/ice_rubble_microstructure_study.py --cases mu_1.00,fill_1200
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/ice_rubble_microstructure_study.py --analyse
PYTHONPATH=src python -m pytest tests/test_ice_rubble_microstructure.py -q
```

Each case takes 1-2 minutes of single-core CPU; the driver writes one JSON per case and
skips cases that already exist, so the sweep can be sharded over processes or cluster nodes.
