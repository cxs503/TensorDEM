## 1. What was missing

`docs/dem3d_lattice_elasticity.md` calibrated the *statics* of the 13-neighbour
simple-cubic bond network and bounded the explicit step from the phonon spectrum,
but stated that this bound "is a necessary condition only: it says the chosen step
cannot be rejected by the elastic bound, **not that the dynamics is accurate**".
No test in the repository pushed a travelling or oscillating disturbance through
`IceDEM3D` and compared it with a reference, and the phonon bound itself was
computed with a dynamical matrix that counted each bond class once.

This document adds the missing dynamic layer and repays the associated defect.

## 2. Reference theory

For a central-force lattice the harmonic equation of motion gives

    D(q) = (1/m) sum_b k (1 - cos(q.r_b)) n_b (x) n_b ,   omega^2 = eig D(q)

evaluated over the 26 bond directions the solver really builds (3 axis, 6
face-diagonal, 4 body-diagonal classes, each realised in both signs). For
`q = (k,0,0)` the sum is analytic:

    omega_L^2 = (26/3) (k/m) (1 - cos k a),   omega_T^2 = (14/3) (k/m) (1 - cos k a)

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| bond stiffness k | 2000 N/m |
| particle radius R | 0.025 m |
| lattice spacing a = 2R | 0.05 m |
| particle mass m | 0.0600175 kg |
| lattice density rho = m/a^3 | 480.14 kg/m^3 |

Long-wavelength limits implied by the calibrated elastic constants of
`dem3d_lattice_elasticity.md` (C11 = 13/3 k/a = 173333 Pa, C12 = C44 = 7/3 k/a, E100 = 108000 Pa):

| speed | value [m/s] | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sqrt(C11/rho) | 19.000152 | bulk longitudinal, laterally constrained |
| sqrt(C44/rho) | 13.942300 | shear |
| sqrt(E100/rho) | 14.997812 | slender bar, free lateral surfaces |

## 4. Defect repaid: the phonon bound was under-reported by sqrt(2)

`tensordem.lattice_elasticity.max_phonon_frequency` summed
`D(q) = (1/m) sum_b k (1 - cos) n n` over the **13 half-space classes** only.
The `-r_b` partner of every class carries the same `1 - cos` weight, so the
summed matrix was half of the physical one and every frequency came out `sqrt(2)`
too small, which in turn over-stated `dt_crit = 2/omega_max` by the same factor.

| quantity | prior (published) | corrected | closed form (zone edge) |
|---|---|---|---|
| omega_max [rad/s] | 537.405441 | 760.006063 | 760.006063 |
| dt_crit = 2/omega_max [s] | 3.721585e-03 | 2.631558e-03 | - |
| recommended_dt / dt_crit | 0.0303 | 0.0429 | - |

The under-report factor is `0.707107` (= 1/sqrt 2) and
the corrected value matches the closed-form zone-edge frequency to `1.50e-16`.

Direction of the error matters: `recommended_dt` stays safely below the
corrected bound, so no existing simulation became unstable, but anyone who had
*set* a step at `0.9 * dt_crit` from the reported value would have run ~1.41x above
the central-difference bound of the bonded lattice. `docs/dem3d_lattice_elasticity.md`
section 3.4 is regenerated by its own script with this fix.

## 5. End-to-end dynamic response against the exact modal solution

A Gaussian axial displacement packet (amplitude 1 um, far below the 15 mm
breaking strain) is released in a free-lateral bar and the cross-section-mean
`v_x` is recorded at two stations (upstream station at least ~3 packet widths
away from the excitation, so no near-field artefact enters).

The reference is *not* a 1-D dispersion approximation: it is the exact harmonic
solution of the same finite discrete system,

    u(t) = sum_m phi_m (phi_m . u0) cos(omega_m t),

built from every eigenpair of the stiffness matrix assembled out of the
solver's own bond list, so mode conversion between the longitudinal branch and
the cross-section resonances - which dominates a 2x2-particle bar - is included.
A mismatch can then come only from the force assembly, the neighbour search, the
fracture bookkeeping or the integrator, not from an idealised reference.

### 5.1 Why a crest-speed comparison is *not* the right acceptance test here

The obvious check - crest travel time against `sqrt(E100/rho)` - fails for two
structural reasons that are properties of the model, not bugs:

1. **Surface deficiency.** A 2x2-particle cross-section is dominated by surface
   particles, so its bar modulus is well below `E100` (the same effect quantified
   on affine strain in `dem3d_lattice_elasticity.md` section 3.3). The
   free-lateral Bloch branch of that cross-section travels at
   11.874 m/s long-wave, not at the
   14.998 m/s slender-bar value.
2. **Cross-section resonances.** Even so, a packet in a 2x2 bar couples to the
   k-independent thickness resonances of the cross-section, whose group velocity
   is nearly zero; the recorded trace is a beat of a fast low-frequency front and
   a trapped high-frequency component. A single-number crest speed is then not
   well defined: repeated trials of this benchmark moved the apparent speed by tens
   of percent with the packet width, which is exactly why this document uses the
   full trace against the exact modal solution instead, and leaves absolute wave
   speeds to the analytic dispersion section where the 3-D bulk branches are
   verified against `C11`/`C44` to `1e-8`.

## 9. Caveats

- The reference is the *harmonic* lattice: bond failure, contact and rolling
  resistance are never exercised, because the packet amplitude is 1 um.
- Contacts never activate (the spheres exactly touch), so the contact spring is
  set to 100 N/m without damping purely to relax the explicit step
  guard; the bonded branch binds the step at the recorded `dt`.
- The Bloch reference assumes periodic x; the simulated bar has free ends, so
  the comparison uses the mid-bar stations before any reflection arrives.
- Both runs are slender cross-sections (2x2 and 4x4 particles); a truly 3-D bulk
  plane wave would need a much thicker sample and a different reference.
- The modal reference shares the stiffness matrix with the solver's bond list, so
  it verifies the dynamic path (assembly, search, integrator, bookkeeping) and not
  the bond law itself; the bond law is anchored by the analytic section and by
  `docs/dem3d_lattice_elasticity.md`.
